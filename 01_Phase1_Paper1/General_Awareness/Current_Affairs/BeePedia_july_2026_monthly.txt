RBI's New Integrated Ombudsman Scheme Comes into Effect from July 1 
KEY POINTS: 
• The Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) revamped Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 
came into effect on July 1, 2026, replacing the 2021 framework. 
• The new scheme provides a faster, cost-free, and non-adversarial mechanism for 
resolving customer complaints against banks, eligible NBFCs, prepaid payment 
instrument (PPI) issuers, and credit information companies. 
• The scheme retains the "One Nation, One Ombudsman"  approach, meaning 
complaints are handled without regard to the location of the customer or the 
regulated entity. 
• Customers must first approach the concerned regulated entity. If the entity fails to 
respond within 30 days or if the customer is dissatisfied, the complaint can be 
escalated to the RBI Ombudsman within 90 days. 
• The Ombudsman can pass an award directing the regulated entity to take remedial 
action or pay compensation. The scheme allows compensation of up to ₹30 lakh 
for consequential losses and up to ₹3 lakh for loss of time, expenses, harassment, 
and mental anguish. 
• Awards passed by the Ombudsman can be appealed before the Appellate 
Authority within prescribed timelines. 
 
World Bank to Phase Out Lending to China by 2031 
KEY POINTS: 
• The World Bank plans to end its lending to China by 2031 as part of a new five -
year "country partnership framework", reflecting China's rise as the world's 
second-largest economy. 
• World Bank lending to China has declined steadily from $2.4 billion per year  in 
2017 to $750 million in 2025. 
• The new framework will limit lending to China to $2 billion between now and 2031, 
ending thereafter. 
• China exited eligibility for loans under the World Bank's International 
Development Association (IDA) facility for the poorest countries in 2000. It began 
contributing to the facility in 2007 and is now the fifth-biggest donor. 
• The US and other countries have long pushed the World Bank to stop lending to 
China given its growing economic power. The US Treasury called the move "a step 
in the right direction." 
• China's finance ministry stated that the gradual decline in loans is a natural result 
of changes in domestic demand and transformation of cooperation, adding that

GDP Calculations Get New Yardstick with PPI Deflator from June Quarter 
KEY POINTS: 
• The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (Mospi) will introduce 
the Producer Price Index (PPI) as the deflator for calculating real GDP from the 
June quarter of FY27, replacing the Wholesale Price Index (WPI). 
• The statistics ministry will release revised real GDP estimates for previous years 
(2022-23, 2023-24, and 2024-25) using the output PPI on August 31, alongside 
the April-June quarter GDP data. 
• The move follows the shift from WPI to output PPI as the deflator in the Index of 
Industrial Production (IIP) data released on June 29. India's first PPI framework 
was unveiled on June 15 by DPIIT. 
• An analysis showed that FY25 IIP growth was revised down from 6.4% under WPI 
deflator to 5.7% under output PPI, while FY26 growth remained unchanged. 
• Economists suggest the impact on GDP will be limited because only a small share 
of manufacturing Gross Value Added (GVA) is derived from IIP; most comes from 
listed company performance. 
 
ICICI Bank Plans $500 Million Dollar Bond Issue After Nearly a Decade 
KEY POINTS: 
• ICICI Bank is planning to raise $500 million through overseas dollar bonds under 
its Global Medium -Term Note (GMTN) programme, marking its first benchmark -
sized dollar bond issuance since 2017. 
• The bank will become the fourth commercial bank  to utilize the RBI's 
concessional swap window for overseas foreign currency borrowing, following 
HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, and State Bank of India (SBI). 
• The RBI introduced the concessional swap window last month to lower hedging 
costs for foreign currency bond issuances, attracting foreign capital and supporting 
the rupee. 
• Under the facility, the RBI undertakes swaps at a fixed rate of 1.5% per annum, 
compounded semi-annually, for eligible overseas foreign currency borrowings with 
a minimum maturity of three years. 
• The process is expected to begin after the bank announces its April-June quarter 
earnings in the third week of July, with the issuance likely to take 30-35 days from 
now. 
• Investors have been demanding higher premiums on Indian bond issuances due 
to anticipated supply, with spreads widening on recent dollar bond issues.

RBI Flags Nascent Stress in Micro Enterprises; Retail Loans Need Monitoring 
KEY POINTS: 
• The RBI's Financial Stability Report noted that while overall asset quality in the 
MSME segment remains benign, there are nascent signs of stress  visible in 
micro enterprises that require attention. 
• Household debt continued to rise, reaching 45.5% of GDP at the end of September 
2025, driven mainly by non-housing retail loans which accounted for 58.4% of total 
household borrowings as of March 2026. 
• Gross NPA ratios in secured and unsecured retail loans stood at 0.7% and 1.7% 
respectively as of end -March 2026, but the report said these segments require 
close monitoring as risks could increase if economic conditions weaken. 
• Banks face funding pressure as their liability profile shifts from low -cost CASA 
deposits to higher-cost term deposits and certificates of deposit, increasing the 
marginal cost of funds and impacting profitability. 
• Banks are expanding credit to higher -yielding segments like MSME and retail to 
safeguard margins. Banks that increased MSME and retail lending experienced 
relatively muted pressure on net interest margins. 
• The share of housing loans of ₹50 lakh and above  increased to 44.7% of 
outstanding housing credit, while the gross NPA ratio in housing loans declined to 
0.5% in March 2026 from 1.2% in March 2019.

record-high surplus transfer of ₹2.87 trillion  from the RBI, marking the third 
consecutive year of May surplus due to the central bank's dividend.

• The RBI has revised the application form and indicative checklist for NBFCs and 
Housing Finance Companies (HFCs) seeking voluntary surrender of their 
Certificate of Registration (CoR).

application along with prescribed documents through the PRAVAAH portal.

incorporate changes arising from the new regulatory framework for Unregistered 
Type I NBFCs. 
• Submission of an application should not be construed as cancellation of 
registration; entities must continue to comply with all RBI and NHB regulations and 
file supervisory returns until the cancellation is formally approved and 
communicated. 
• The Certificate of Registration, issued under the RBI Act, 1934, is mandatory for 
companies undertaking NBFC activities.

with southern states like Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu recording 
the highest rates.

RBI Proposes One-Time Approval for MFs and Insurers to Raise Bank Stakes 
Key Points: 
• RBI has proposed a one -time approval mechanism for mutual funds, insurance 
companies, and pension funds to acquire major shareholding (5% or more) in a 
bank, replacing the need for repeated approvals. 
• Under the existing framework, these entities had to seek fresh RBI approval each 
time their holding fell below 5% and they wanted to acquire again. 
• The one-time approval will cover future acquisitions of up to 10% of the paid -up 
share capital or voting rights in the same bank, subject to conditions. 
• The approval will remain valid unless revoked by RBI, even if the investor's holding 
later falls below the 5% threshold. 
• Entities must notify RBI and the concerned bank within one day if their aggregate 
holding crosses above or falls below the 5% threshold.

Federation of India (CPSFI) signed an MoU to jointly bid for the 2029 World 
Abilitysport Games. 
• Chennai and Bhubaneswar are being considered as potential host cities. 
• The Games are expected to feature over 1,500 athletes across multiple para-
sports disciplines. 
• Hosting the event would strengthen India's position in the global Paralympic

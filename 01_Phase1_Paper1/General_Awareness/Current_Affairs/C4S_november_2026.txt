Surangam: Kerala's Traditional Water Harvesting System
Source: The Hindu
Context of the News
Eight grama panchayats in Kerala's Kasaragod district are preparing to restore traditional Surangam
underground water tunnels, to strengthen groundwater conservation, rainwater harvesting and
drought resilience.
What Is a Surangam?
An indigenous underground water-harvesting system
Found mainly in Kasaragod district, Kerala
It consists of horizontal tunnels dug into laterite hills
These collect groundwater that seeps naturally through the rock
How Does It Work?
The tunnels
Narrow, horizontal passages cut into laterite hills
They intercept seeping groundwater and guide it to collection points
No pumping is involved
Gravity does the work
The passages slope gently downward
Water flows out on its own
No electricity, pumps or complex equipment needed
The seasonal cycle
During the monsoon, rainwater soaks into the laterite
This recharges underground reserves
Those reserves then feed the surangams through the dry summer
Important Facts for Exams
Surangam — horizontal tunnels dug into laterite hills, found in Kasaragod district, Kerala
Eight grama panchayats are restoring them
Works by gravity, with no pumps or electricity
Recharged by the monsoon; supplies water through the dry summer
It is also found in parts of Dakshina Kannada, Karnataka, where it is called thurangam or suranga

New Protein Discovered in the Plant Defence Alarm System
Scientists at the National Institute of Plant Genome Research (NIPGR), New Delhi, discovered a protein
called CNGC13, which helps plants transmit warning signals when insects attack their leaves.
When an insect feeds on one leaf, the plant sends an alarm signal to other leaves, helping them
prepare their defence.
Earlier, Glutamate Receptor-Like Channels (GLRs) were considered important in transmitting such
signals. The new study identifies CNGC13 as another protein involved in this process.
CNGC13 is mainly found in the xylem and phloem, the plant tissues that transport water and food. It
allows calcium to enter cells as the alarm signal moves through the plant.
Decent Work in India
India's unemployment rate declined from 6.1% in 2017–18 to 3.2% in 2023–24, while female workforce
participation also increased.
However, the quality of employment remains important. The concept of decent work, defined by the
International Labour Organization (ILO), focuses on fair income, workplace rights, social security and
dignity.
The study used Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) data for 2017–18 and 2023–24 to develop a
multidimensional Decent Work Index (DWI).
The index considered three dimensions: employment status, employment opportunity and social
security, across agriculture, manufacturing, construction, wholesale and retail, and transport.

CBIC Notifies Key GST Changes under Finance Act, 2026
In October 2026, the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) notified key Goods and
Services Tax (GST) amendments under the Finance Act, 2026, effective from 1 November 2026.
Post-Supply Discounts: Amended Section 15(3)(b) allows post-supply discounts to be excluded from
the value of supply if the supplier issues a credit note and the recipient reverses the corresponding
Input Tax Credit (ITC). Prior agreement and strict invoice-wise linkage are no longer required.
Provisional Refunds: Amended Section 54(6) extends provisional refunds to eligible claims under the
inverted duty structure. Businesses can receive 90% of eligible refunds within seven days, subject to
conditions.
Export Refunds: Amended Section 54(14) removes the minimum ₹1,000 refund restriction for tax paid
on goods exported out of India, benefiting small exporters.

108 Stupas in Bhutan with India-Linked Buddha Relics
Source: The Hindu
Context of the News
Buddhist relics of Indian origin will be placed in most of the 108 jangchub chortens being built along a 12
km stretch of the Mau Chhu River in Gelephu, Bhutan.
What Is Project 108?
Bhutan will build 108 stupas at the same time
They are called jangchub chortens, which means stupas of enlightenment
The work marks the start of the Gelephu Mindfulness City
King Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuk wants this city to be a business hub built around spiritual
life
Gelephu is about 240 km from the capital Thimphu. It touches Assam’s Chirang district
What Does a Jangchub Chorten Mean?
It marks the moment the Buddha gained enlightenment
It stands for wisdom winning over ignorance
Once the rituals are done, the stupa is treated as equal to the living Buddha
Key Terms
Stupa / chorten: A dome-shaped Buddhist structure, usually holding relics
Relic: A small sacred remain of the Buddha or a holy person
Zung: The sacred items placed inside a stupa
Thangka: A Buddhist painting made on cloth or silk
Dharani: A rolled paper with mantras written on it
Mala: A prayer string with 108 beads
Kangyur: The collection of Buddha’s teachings in Tibetan Buddhism

The NVS-03 Satellite
Source: The Hindu
Context of the News
ISRO launched the NVS-03 satellite on 30 October 2026. It weighs 2,250 kg. The rocket used was GSLV-
F18, and it took off from Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota.
What Is NVS-03?
The third satellite in India's second-generation navigation fleet
The series is called NVS
It supports India's own navigation system, NavIC
NavIC was earlier known as IRNSS
What Will It Do?
It gives position, navigation and timing services
This means it tells you where you are, which way to go, and the exact time
It covers the whole of India
It also covers an area up to 1,500 km beyond India's borders
Where Was It Placed?
First into a Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit
This is a temporary oval-shaped orbit
The satellite will later raise itself into its final round orbit
Important Facts for Exams
NVS-03 launched on 30 October 2026 by GSLV-F18 from Sriharikota
Weight 2,250 kg; mission life 12 years
It is the third satellite in the second-generation NVS series
Carries an Indian-made rubidium atomic clock
The full system has seven satellites — three geostationary and four geosynchronous
The plan is for five second-generation satellites, NVS-01 to NVS-05
India started this project after the 1999 Kargil war, when it was refused GPS data
Glacial Lake Outburst Floods
Source: Indian Express
Context of the News
A glacial lake outburst flood at Shintaphu Lake in northwestern Bhutan caused sudden flash floods
along the Mo Chhu and Sankosh river systems. India's Central Water Commission and district
administrations issued flash-flood warnings in border districts of West Bengal and Assam.
What Is a GLOF?
A sudden, high-speed release of water from a glacial lake
The lake is held back by unstable natural barriers — moraines (loose rock and sediment), ice, or
bedrock
When that barrier gives way, the water escapes all at once

Important Days
World Vegan Day 2026 – 1 November
World Vegan Day is observed every year on 1 November to promote veganism, animal welfare and the
benefits of a vegan lifestyle.
The day was established in 1994 by Louise Wallis, then Chair of The Vegan Society, to mark the
organisation's 50th anniversary.
The first World Vegan Day was observed on 1 November 1994.
The term “vegan” was coined by Donald Watson in November 1944. It was formed from the first three
and last two letters of the word “vegetarian”.

In October 2026, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) extended the Know Your Customer

State of the Cryosphere 2026
Source: Down To Earth
Context of the News
Before COP31 at Antalya, two reports were released by the International Cryosphere Climate Initiative
(ICCI) and UNEP:
State of the Cryosphere 2026: The Cost of Overshoot
Overshoot and the Cryosphere: Averting Global Meltdown
They warn that even crossing 1.5°C for a short time could cause permanent ice loss and release huge
amounts of carbon from frozen soil.
What Is the Cryosphere?
It means all the frozen water on earth. This includes:
Ice sheets — the huge ice layers on Greenland and Antarctica
Mountain glaciers
Seasonal snow
Sea ice
Ice on lakes and rivers
Permafrost — soil that stays frozen all year
What Did the Report Find?
Faster Warming: The Arctic is warming four times faster than the global average. Hindu Kush
Himalayas warm by 1.8–2.2°C for every 1.5°C of global warming.
Permafrost Carbon: Permafrost covers around 14 million sq. km and stores nearly 1,700 billion tonnes
of carbon. Thawing releases carbon dioxide and methane.
Rapid Ice Loss: Greenland and Antarctica are losing ice six times faster than in the 1990s.
Sea-Level Rise: At 2.8–3.2°C warming, sea levels could rise by 5 cm annually by 2150.
Glacier Loss: At 3°C warming, most mid-latitude glaciers below 6,000 metres could disappear, along
with two-thirds of Hindu Kush Himalayan ice.
Permafrost Emissions: Thawing could release greenhouse gases equivalent to the United States'
annual emissions for 100–200 years.
AMOC Disruption: Ice-free Arctic summers and freshwater entering the North Atlantic could weaken
the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC), affecting European temperatures and global
climate.

Why Is the Cryosphere Melting?

The 1.5°C overshoot refers to a situation in which global temperatures temporarily rise above the 1.5°C

immediate emission cuts, while placing a greater burden on developing countries.

World Urbanism Day 2026 – 8 November
World Urbanism Day (WUD), also known as World Town Planning Day, was observed on 8 November
2026.
It highlights the importance of urban and regional planning in developing sustainable, inclusive and
liveable cities.
The observance was initiated in 1949 by Professor Carlos María della Paolera of the University of
Buenos Aires, Argentina.

World Immunization Day 2026 – 10 November
World Immunization Day is observed annually on 10 November to promote awareness about
vaccination and its role in preventing diseases.
The observance supports the WHO's Immunization Agenda 2030 (IA2030), which aims to ensure
access to vaccines for people of all age groups.
The WHO launched the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) in 1974 to expand access to life-
saving vaccines, particularly for children.
The first World Immunization Day was observed on 10 November 2012.

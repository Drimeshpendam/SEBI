RBI Banking and Economy 
RBI proposes ₹100 crore limit on bank loans for agri infrastructure under 
revised PSL norms 
Key Points 
• The RBI proposed an aggregate sanctioned limit of ₹100 crore per borrower 
from the banking system for agricultural infrastructure  loans, under draft 
Priority Sector Lending (PSL) norms.  
– For crop loans, medium and long -term agri loans and pre - and post -
harvest loans, the proposed limit is ₹4 crore per borrowing entity. 
• Education loans to individuals, including vocational courses, up to ₹25 lakh 
will be eligible for PSL. 
• Renewable energy  loans up to ₹35 crore  for power generators and public 
utilities, and up to ₹10 lakh per borrower for households, will be eligible. 
• The RBI also issued draft directions for small finance banks, local area banks 
and regional rural banks, and invited comments by 26 November. 
Q: What is Priority Sector Lending (PSL)?  A: It is an RBI requirement that banks lend 
a set share of their credit to sectors like agriculture, MSMEs, education and housing, 
which may otherwise get inadequate credit. 
Q: What is a draft direction?  A: It is a proposed regulation that the RBI releases for 
public feedback before issuing the final rules. 
 
RBI tightens cyber oversight of non-bank payment system operators 
Key Points 
• The RBI issued consolidated Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and 
Digital Payment Security Controls  for non-bank payment system 
operators (PSOs), including NPCI, card networks, payment aggregators, PPI 
issuers and white-label ATM operators (WLAOs).  
– They take effect immediately for large and medium PSOs, and small 
PSOs have until 1 April 2028. 
– Six earlier circulars, the oldest from 2009, have been repealed. 
• PSOs must obtain the source code of critical third-party applications, or have 
an escrow arrangement in place.  
– Otherwise, they need a developer certificate that the code is free of 
vulnerabilities, malware and covert channels, renewed after every 
change. 
• PSOs must file an annual system audit report  within two months  of the 
financial year-end.  
– The auditor must not have provided any service to the entity in the last 
two financial years , and open observations must be fixed within 3 
months.

• Cyber incidents must be reported to the RBI within six hours of detection, and 
a minimum 12-hour cooling-off period applies after a customer changes their 
registered mobile number or email. 
Q: What is a Payment System Operator (PSO)?  A: A PSO is an entity authorised by 
the RBI to set up and operate a payment system, such as a payment aggregator or 
prepaid instrument issuer. 
Q: What is a white-label ATM operator (WLAO)?  A: It is a non-bank entity authorised 
by the RBI to set up and run ATMs that serve customers of all banks. 
 
RBI Constitutes TCCFM, Eases Reporting for Small Service Exporters and

• RBI constituted a Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets 
(TCCFM), chaired by Deputy Governor Rohit Jain, to engage market

RBI's net short dollar forward position hits record $239.2 billion in September 
Key Points 
• The RBI's outstanding net short dollar position in the forward market rose to 
a record $239.2 billion  at the end of September, from $200.06 billion  in 
August.  
– Net shorts of more than one year maturity rose to $199 billion from 
$176.6 billion. 
• The rise was driven by the special concessional swap window for banks to 
mobilise FCNR(B) deposits, which was operationalised on 8 June.  
– The window closed on 31 August, but banks could enter swaps under it 
until 11 September. 
– Banks mobilised $133 billion in FCNR(B) deposits through the window. 
• The 12-month dollar -rupee forward premium  rose by over 120 bps  in 
October, to 4.57% from 3.36%. 
• The rupee rebounded about 2% from its recent low of 96.85 per dollar. 
Q: What is a net short dollar forward position?  A: It means the RBI has contracted to 
sell more dollars than it has bought in the forward market, to be delivered on future 
dates. 
Q: What is a forward premium?  A: It is the difference between the forward and spot 
exchange rates, which reflects the cost of hedging future dollar payments.

Inward FDI to ODI ratio narrows to 4.2 times in FY26 from 5.5 times: RBI 
Key Points 
• The ratio of India's outstanding inward FDI to ODI narrowed to 4.2 times at the 
end of March 2026 from 5.5 times a year earlier, according to the RBI's annual 
census on Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA). 
• The US was the largest source of outstanding FDI in India at 16.5%, followed 
by Mauritius (14.5%), Singapore (13.7%) and the UK (10.8%). 
• For ODI, Singapore, the UAE and the US were the top destinations, together 
accounting for more than half of Indian entities' overseas investments. 
• Manufacturing accounted for 50.1% of outstanding FDI equity capital at 
market value, followed by services at 45%.  
– About 97% of Indian entities reporting FDI and/or ODI were unlisted. 
Q: What is Overseas Direct Investment (ODI)?  A: It is an investment by an Indian 
entity in a foreign entity, such as setting up or acquiring a business abroad. 
Q: What is the FLA census?  A: The Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA) census is the 
RBI's annual survey that captures the cross -border assets and liabilities of Indian 
entities.

India's manufacturing PMI dips to 54.7 in October 
Key Points 
• The HSBC India Manufacturing PMI  fell to 54.7 in October from 55.1 in 
September, slightly below the flash estimate of 54.9.  
– A reading above 50 indicates expansion, and October was the 60th 
consecutive month of expansion. 
• New export orders grew at their fastest pace in six months, while growth in 
new domestic business eased from September's seven-month high. 
• Input cost inflation  fell to its lowest since February, and the rise in selling 
prices was the weakest in just under four years. 
• Factory employment rose, but job creation slowed and capital goods  
employment declined. 
Q: What is the PMI?  A: The Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) is a monthly survey -
based indicator of business activity, where above 50 means expansion and below 50 
means contraction. 
Q: What is a flash PMI?  A: It is an early estimate of the PMI released before the final 
data, based on a subset of survey responses.

SEBI and Financial Awareness 
Sebi eases digital KYC rules for NRIs and foreign nationals from December 3 
Key Points 
• Sebi relaxed KYC norms for persons resident outside India (PROIs), letting 
those in FATF-compliant countries complete digital onboarding without being 
physically present in India.  
– The framework is effective from 3 December and covers NRIs, OCIs 
and foreign nationals. 
– Clients in FATF non-compliant countries  continue to follow existing 
KYC requirements. 
• KYC records are now portable across intermediaries, and intermediaries can 
rely on KYC done by another Sebi -registered intermediary, based on records 
from KRAs or the Central KYC Records Registry. 
• Intermediaries must collect passport copies and, where applicable, OCI cards, 
and overseas addresses will be accepted as proof of address.  
– Video-based in-person verification requires an authorised official and 
is subject to concurrent audit. 
• Intermediaries must submit completed KYC information to KRAs within three 
working days. 
Q: What is the FATF?  A: The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) is the global body 
that sets standards to combat money laundering and terrorist financing. 
Q: What is a KYC Registration Agency (KRA)?  A: A KRA is a Sebi -registered entity 
that stores investors' KYC records so that they can be reused across financial 
intermediaries.

SEBI Extends KYC Review Periodicity to 8 Years for Eligible FPIs 
• SEBI's circular "Rationalisation of periodicity of KYC review for FPIs" extends 
the KYC review period for eligible FPIs from 3 years to 8 years, to ease 
compliance. It takes effect from 1 December 2026. 
• The annual KYC review period is extended from 1 year to 2 years for specified 
FPIs. 
• Category I: 8 years for all FPIs from non -high-risk jurisdictions and those 
registered under Regulation 5(a)(i) from high-risk jurisdictions. Other Category 
I FPIs from high-risk jurisdictions are reviewed every 2 years. 
• Category II: 8 years for regulated FPIs from non -high-risk jurisdictions. All 
others, including all from high-risk jurisdictions, are reviewed every 2 years. 
 
Sebi allows mutual funds to net cash market fund obligations from April 2027 
Key Points 
• Sebi allowed mutual fund schemes  to net fund obligations  from outright 
purchase and sale transactions in the cash market, effective 1 April 2027.  
– It extends to mutual funds a net settlement facility  that Sebi earlier 
introduced for FPIs. 
– The aim is to reduce temporary funding requirements  and improve 
settlement efficiency. 
• Netting is allowed only within the same scheme and settlement cycle , and 
not across different schemes of the same fund.  
– If a scheme buys and sells the same security in the same cycle, it is 
settled on a gross basis. 
• Securities settlement continues on a gross basis , and derivatives, 
securities lending and borrowing  and short selling  transactions remain 
outside the framework. 
• Sebi asked AMFI to formulate implementation standards, and AMCs and 
custodians must ensure scheme-wise accounting and audit trails. 
Q: What is net settlement?  A: It is a settlement method where amounts payable and 
receivable are offset, so only the net difference is paid or received. 
Q: What is gross settlement?  A: It is a settlement method where each transaction is 
settled separately and in full, without offsetting.

Sebi eases accredited investor norms, lets fund managers certify status 
Key Points 
• Sebi allowed managers of AIFs, asset management companies of SIFs and 
portfolio managers to verify and grant accredited investor status, in addition 
to the existing route through recognised accreditation agencies.  
– The circular came into effect immediately. 
• Such accreditation is valid for three years and is recognised across managers 
in the same corporate group.  
– Managers must keep verification records for at least five years. 
• LLPs qualify as accredited investors if each partner independently meets the 
eligibility criteria. 
• Persons resident outside India are treated as deemed accredited investors 
and need no separate certificate, though AML and client due diligence  
obligations remain. 
Q: What is an accredited investor?  A: It is an investor who meets prescribed income 
or net worth criteria and is considered capable of taking higher risks, so they can invest 
in certain products with relaxed norms. 
Q: What is an AIF?  A: An Alternative Investment Fund (AIF) is a privately pooled 
investment vehicle, such as a venture capital or private equity fund, that raises money 
from sophisticated investors. 
 
Sebi cuts OFS cooling-off period for illiquid privately listed InvITs to 8 weeks 
Key Points 
• Sebi reduced the cooling-off period for offer-for-sale (OFS) transactions by 
privately listed illiquid InvITs  to 8 weeks  from 12 weeks , effective 
immediately.  
– The aim is to ease doing business and trading-related restrictions. 
• The cooling-off period covers purchases or sales before and after an OFS, and 
depends on liquidity.  
– It is 2 weeks for the most liquid shares, 4 weeks for liquid shares and 
12 weeks for illiquid shares. 
• Privately listed InvITs face liquidity constraints due to their high trading lot size, 
which limits participation largely to institutional investors, body corporates 
and HNIs. 
Q: What is an InvIT?  A: An Infrastructure Investment Trust (InvIT) is a trust that pools 
money from investors to own and operate infrastructure assets, such as roads and 
power transmission lines, and pays them returns. 
Q: What is an Offer for Sale (OFS)?  A: It is a mechanism where existing shareholders, 
such as promoters, sell their shares to the public through the stock exchange platform.

Nobel Laureate Economist William Nordhaus Passes Away at 85 
• William D. Nordhaus, the American economist who pioneered the economic 
approach to climate change, passed away on October 30, 2026, at the age of 
85. 
• He shared the 2018 Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences with Paul Romer for 
integrating climate change into long-run macroeconomic analysis. 
• He developed the Dynamic Integrated Climate -Economy (DICE) Model, which 
links economic growth, carbon emissions and global temperatures to assess 
the economic costs of climate change and emission reduction. 
• Nordhaus's work laid the foundation for carbon pricing, arguing that polluters 
should pay for environmental damage. In 2017, he estimated the optimal 
carbon tax at $30 per tonne of carbon dioxide. 
• He also proposed the concept of a Climate Club, under which participating 
countries adopt a common carbon price and impose tariffs on imports from 
countries that do not participate.

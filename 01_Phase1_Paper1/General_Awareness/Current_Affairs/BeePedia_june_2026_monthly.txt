RBI Banking and Economy 
RBI holds repo rate at 5.25%, cuts FY27 growth forecast to 6.6% and raises 
inflation forecast to 5.1% 
• The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) unanimously decided to keep the policy repo 
rate unchanged at 5.25% for the third straight time. 
• The central bank has lowered India’s real GDP growth forecast for FY27 from 6.9% 
to 6.6%. 
• CPI inflation forecast for FY27 has been raised sharply from 4.6% to 5.1%, mainly 
due to rising fuel and input prices. 
• Inflation for the October-December quarter is now expected to reach 5.9%, close to 
the upper tolerance limit of 6%. 
• RBI revised its assumed average crude oil price to $95 per barrel for its projections, 
up from $85 in April. 
• The MPC retained its “neutral” stance, meaning it is not committed to either a rate 
hike or a cut and will decide based on incoming data. 
• RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said the central bank will not target any specific 
exchange rate level but will act to curb excessive volatility in the currency market. 
• Rising domestic fuel prices, commercial LPG prices, and global supply chain 
disruptions are seen as major upside risks to inflation.

RBI Opens Concessional Dollar-Rupee Swap Window for PSUs and Banks till December 
2026 
Key Points:  
• The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has operationalised a concessional forex -swap 
facility for public -sector undertakings (PSUs) and banks to encourage overseas 
borrowings and support foreign capital inflows. 
• The swap rate is fixed at 1.5% per annum, compounded semi -annually, which is 
cheaper than market rates. 
• The facility is available for external commercial borrowings (ECB) by PSUs and 
overseas foreign-currency borrowing (OFCB) by banks, with a minimum average 
maturity of three years. 
• Eligible borrowings must be drawn down by December 31, 2026, and the swap 
window will remain open for applications until January 15, 2027. 
• The maximum swap tenor will match the borrowing's repayment schedule, capped 
at five years. 
• The facility is not available for borrowings used for refinancing or repaying existing 
foreign-currency loans. 
• Banks can exclude positions from this swap facility while calculating their net 
overnight open position (NOP) in rupees, giving them more operational flexibility.

Key Points:

Government Announces Measures to Deepen G -Sec Market and Facilitate Greater FPI 
Participation 
Key Points:  
• The Ministry of Finance introduced reforms in June 2026 to increase Foreign 
Portfolio Investor (FPI) participation in Government Securities (G-Secs). 
• The key measures include tax exemptions on interest income, long -term capital 
gains (LTCG), and short-term capital gains (STCG) from G-Sec investments. 
• Under the new regime, FPIs will pay nil tax on interest income earned from G-Secs 
and on capital gains arising from sale, transfer, exchange, or redemption of G -

was taxed at 12.5%. 
• The reforms also include expansion of specified securities under the Fully 
Accessible Route (FAR) to include all new issuances of 15-year, 30-year, and 40-
year government securities. 
• The measures aim to attract stable long -term foreign capital, deepen the G -Sec 
market, and strengthen India's debt market by broadening the investor base.
